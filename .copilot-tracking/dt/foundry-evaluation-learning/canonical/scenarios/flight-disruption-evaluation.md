# Learn evaluation with a flight-disruption assistant

## Learning scenario

### Description

A learner uses Microsoft Foundry to evaluate a passenger-facing flight-disruption assistant. Supplied synthetic answers and fictional airline policy provide a bounded scenario for built-in checks, generated rubrics, and guided quality changes.

### Scenario Narrative

The intended learner is a consultant, delivery engineer, or solution architect with basic Foundry knowledge. The lab gives these roles equal priority and does not require Python authoring.

The learner first runs a simple built-in evaluator and inspects its result. The lab explains what matters in the scenario. The learner then generates a rubric from supplied requirements and policy, reviews the criteria, and evaluates supplied answers. One additional requirement gives the learner a reason to revise and test the rubric.

The intended later exercises compare a live-agent change and investigate evaluation of synthetic live activity. Supplied introductory answers do not satisfy these live requirements. The assistant uses read-only tools; booking execution, claim submission, and legal-entitlement advice are outside scope.

The intended professional value is the ability to connect quality priorities with evidence and explain what that evidence does not establish. Customer alignment and trust are motivations, not measured outcomes. This is a proposed learning scenario grounded in requester decisions, not an observed learner session.
