## Source and privacy boundary

The requester supplied excerpts from two separate practitioner conversations and approved capturing the learning gap. These notes use anonymous source labels. People names, company names, message links, and full transcripts are omitted.

The excerpts were supplied in the coaching conversation. They were not retrieved or independently verified through WorkIQ. Message dates and complete thread context are unavailable. These are two practitioner perspectives, not an industry-wide survey or direct customer evidence.

## Synthesis and tension

Both perspectives connect quality assessment to customer or end-user judgment. They differ in the evaluation practices they describe. Practitioner A reports no formal suite; Practitioner B identifies lifecycle evaluation uses and recommends handover.

Candidate insight: customer judgment and repeatable checks need to work together. Learners need a way to connect customer outcomes to evaluation evidence and explain its limits. The evidence does not justify describing both sources as beginners or concluding that partners generally lack evaluation capability.

The follow-up strengthens evidence of perceived learning value for Practitioner A. The repeated description of current practice adds context, not independent corroboration. A positive response to a suggested lab does not validate the lab design or the wider handover theme.

## User-approved gap

Consultants, delivery engineers, and solution architects need practical ways to define, run, and interpret repeatable evaluations from the start of development and throughout the product lifecycle so they can agree quality requirements, design and build agentic solutions, assess changes against those requirements, and hand over checks and processes for continued use.

Status: accepted by the requester as the learning gap. Supported by two limited practitioner perspectives and the requester's goals, not validated across the intended learner population.

The requester later corrected the consultant-only framing: delivery engineers building solutions and architects designing solutions are equally important audiences with different problems. The expanded statement is a synthesis, not a verbatim practitioner quotation. Detailed role-specific needs remain working interpretations in `problem-definition.md`.

The requester then confirmed the delivery-engineer requirement: adopt evaluations early and evaluate while building, using repeated feedback on changes to verify non-functional requirements throughout product development. They compare this to TDD. This is requester intent, not another practitioner source or evidence of current engineering practice.

The first-run outcome remains introductory: explain what an evaluation is, what it provides for a customer and an agentic AI solution, and why it matters. Independent diagnosis, improvement, and reusable handover remain later track goals.
